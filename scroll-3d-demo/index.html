A scroll-driven WebGL demo

The Scroll
Edition

Scroll down. Every position of the scrollbar is a frame on a keyframed timeline.

Scroll

Scroll scrubs a timeline

Camera paths and object motion are keyframes on one paused timeline. Each frame the page converts scrollY / innerHeight into a time and seeks the timeline there, so scrolling up plays it backwards.

Two scenes, one shader

Each 3D scene renders into its own offscreen texture. A full-screen GLSL pass blends them with a noise-driven edge, so the next world bleeds through like wet paint.

Your mouse nudges the camera

The pointer orbits the camera a few degrees around its keyframed target, eased every frame. The scene feels alive even when you stop scrolling.

Bloom ties it together

An Unreal-style bloom pass runs after the composite. Its strength is a keyframed value too, peaking mid-transition.

How this page is built

  1. Three.js renders two scenes into half-float render targets.
  2. Lenis smooths native scrolling and reports the eased scroll position.
  3. GSAP holds a paused timeline measured in screens scrolled; it is never played, only seeked.
  4. A composite shader mixes the scenes with fractal noise, then bloom and tone mapping run.
  5. When WebGL is missing or reduced motion is requested, the page falls back to a static background.

Add #debug to the URL to see the scroll-to-timeline mapping live.
